Glossary — Marketing Automation

What is
marketing automation?

Marketing automation is the use of software to run repetitive marketing tasks automatically, triggered by rules or AI. Here's the definition, the common examples, and where AI agents take it next.

Marketing automation is the use of software to run repetitive marketing tasks — email sequences, lead nurturing, social posting, ad management, segmentation and reporting — automatically, triggered by rules or by AI, instead of by hand. It lets a small team deliver timely, personalised marketing at a scale that manual work can't match.

Key takeaways

  • Marketing automation runs repetitive marketing tasks automatically, triggered by rules or AI.
  • Common examples: email nurture sequences, lead scoring, segmentation, social scheduling and reporting.
  • The newer wave uses AI agents that pursue goals, not just fixed if-this-then-that rules.
  • For agencies, the biggest wins are SEO research, content production and sales follow-up.

What marketing automation includes

Classic marketing automation covers email marketing and nurture flows, lead capture and scoring, audience segmentation, social media scheduling, ad campaign management, and performance reporting. A rule fires — someone downloads a guide, a lead goes cold, a metric crosses a threshold — and the system acts without a person in the loop.

Rules-based automation vs. AI agents

Traditional automation follows fixed rules you define in advance: if this, then that. AI agents are the next step — you give them a goal and context, and they work out the steps, adapt when conditions change, and handle messier, less predictable work like research, drafting and personalised outreach. Both are "marketing automation"; agents just handle the parts rules can't.

Marketing automation examples

  • Email: a welcome sequence that adapts to what a subscriber clicks.
  • Lead scoring: ranking prospects by fit and behaviour so sales calls the right ones.
  • SEO research: an agent like SAGE running keyword research and reporting on demand.
  • Content: an agent like SCRIBE turning briefs into drafts.
  • Outreach: an agent like NOVA personalising and following up automatically.

Why it matters for agencies

Agencies run marketing across many clients at once, so every manual task multiplies. Automation lets you build a process once and run it across the whole roster. See how in marketing automation for agencies.
